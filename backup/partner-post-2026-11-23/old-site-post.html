This post explains what the certification actually involves, how it's different from a badge an agency puts on itself, and why it's worth paying attention to when you're deciding who builds your site.

First, the news

We're a Webflow Certified Partner now. It's official, and it's on our profile in Webflow's partner directory.

We could leave it there. A lot of agencies do. A new badge on the homepage, a LinkedIn post, and back to work. But most people outside the Webflow world have no idea what the status means, or whether it says anything real about the agency that holds it. So instead of just announcing it, we wanted to explain it. What it takes to get there, what it doesn't, and why it should matter to you if you're about to hand your website to someone.

What Certified Partner actually means

Webflow runs a partner program for agencies and freelancers who build on the platform. There are different tiers, and Certified Partner is the level you reach when Webflow has looked at your work and confirmed you know what you're doing.

It's not self-declared

This is the part most people miss. Being a Webflow Certified Partner isn't something you switch on. You don't pay a fee and get the badge. Webflow reviews the agency, the projects, and the way the work is built before the status is granted. It comes from Webflow, not from us.

That's the whole point of it. Anyone can write "Webflow experts" on their site. The certification exists so there's a version of that claim someone else has actually checked.

It reflects the work, not a weekend course

Some certifications are a video series and a quiz. You watch a few hours of content, answer some questions, and you're certified. That's fine for learning, but it doesn't tell a client much about whether the agency can deliver a real project.

The partner status is tied to the actual builds. It's about how the sites are structured, how they hold up, and whether the work matches the standard Webflow expects from the agencies it puts its name next to. It reflects what we've shipped, not what we've watched.

Why it matters when you're choosing an agency

If you're a marketing lead or a founder about to pick a Webflow agency, you're mostly working with claims. Every agency says it's fast, reliable, and expert. It's hard to tell the difference from the outside until the project is already underway. Here's where the certification helps.

Vetted, not just claimed

The status is a second opinion from the company that made the platform. When Webflow certifies an agency, it's saying the work meets its bar. That doesn't replace your own research, but it's one signal you don't have to take purely on trust, because it didn't come from the agency's own marketing.

A sign of focus

Agencies that reach partner status tend to be the ones that take Webflow seriously as their main platform, not a side offering. That focus usually shows up in the work: cleaner builds, better CMS structures, fewer of the shortcuts you get when Webflow is just one of ten things an agency dabbles in. The certification is a decent proxy for how deep the team actually goes.

It takes risk off the decision

Redoing a website is expensive, slow, and painful. The worst outcome isn't paying a bit more, it's paying for a build that falls apart six months in and having to start over. A vetted partner is one of the ways you lower the odds of that happening. It's not a guarantee, but it narrows the field to teams that have been checked.

What it changes for our clients

For the people we already work with, the honest answer is: not much, and that's on purpose. The certification confirms the way we were already building. It's recognition of the standard, not a switch to a new one.

What it does add is access. Partner status brings us closer to Webflow itself, to early features, to the roadmap, and to support that helps when a project needs something out of the ordinary. That's the part clients feel indirectly, when something that would normally be a workaround turns out to be straightforward because we know the platform and the people behind it.

The work behind it

Webflow is all we do. We don't split our attention across five platforms and treat Webflow as one of them. Every project we take on is built there, which is why the partner status made sense as a next step rather than a stretch.

So the badge is nice, but it isn't the point. The point is the work it reflects, and the standard we hold whether or not there's a badge attached to it. The certification just makes that standard easier for someone on the outside to see before they've worked with us.

If you're weighing up who should build or rebuild your site, that's the value of it. Not the logo, but the fact that someone independent looked at the work first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Webflow Certified Partner?
A Webflow Certified Partner is an agency or freelancer that Webflow has reviewed and approved for its work on the platform. It's a status Webflow grants, not one an agency gives itself, and it appears in Webflow's own partner directory. In short, it's Webflow confirming that the partner meets its standard for building on Webflow.
How does an agency become a Webflow Certified Partner?
It isn't a fee you pay or a quiz you pass. Webflow looks at the agency's actual projects, how the sites are built and structured, and whether the work meets the bar Webflow expects before granting the status. So the certification reflects real, shipped work, not a course someone watched over a weekend.
Why does it matter if my agency is a Webflow Certified Partner?
When you're choosing who builds your site, most of what you have to go on is the agency's own claims. Certification is a second opinion from the company that made the platform, so it's one signal you don't have to take purely on trust. It also tends to mark agencies that focus on Webflow instead of treating it as a side offering, and that usually shows up in cleaner builds and a lower chance of a site that falls apart after launch.
